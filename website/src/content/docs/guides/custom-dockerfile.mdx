Time to complete: about 5 minutes.

Icefall detects your framework and generates a Dockerfile for you, covering
common stacks such as Astro, Next.js, Nuxt, Vite, Node, and static sites. When
you need full control over the build, commit your own `Dockerfile` and Icefall
uses it as-is.

## Prerequisites

- A deployed or new app from a Git repository.

## Use your own Dockerfile

<Steps>

1. Add a `Dockerfile` to the root of your repository.

2. Commit and push it. On the next deploy, Icefall detects the `Dockerfile` and
   builds from it directly instead of generating one.

3. Watch the **Logs** tab during the deploy to confirm your build steps run.

</Steps>

When a `Dockerfile` is present at the repository root, Icefall does not generate
or overwrite it — your build is used exactly as written.

## Adjust build settings

For generated builds you do not want to replace entirely, the app's build
settings let you override pieces such as the framework, base image, build
command, start command, and port — without committing a full `Dockerfile`.

## Verify

<Steps>

1. Trigger a deploy.

2. In the build logs, confirm the steps match your `Dockerfile` (or your
   overridden build settings).

3. Confirm the app starts and passes its health check.

</Steps>

## Troubleshooting

- **Generated build still runs**: confirm the `Dockerfile` is at the repository
  root and was committed to the deploy branch.
- **Build fails**: read the build logs for the failing step. A custom
  `Dockerfile` is your responsibility to keep working.
